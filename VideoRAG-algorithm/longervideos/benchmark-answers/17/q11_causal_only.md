### Criteria Used by the National Park Service in Prioritizing Environmental Cleanup Projects

The National Park Service (NPS) employs a structured approach to prioritize environmental cleanup projects through its Environmental Compliance and Cleanup Division (ECCD). This division utilizes a hierarchical model of criteria to evaluate and fund projects that align with the NPS's overarching goals. Here are the key criteria and subcriteria that guide this process:

#### Main Goals
1. **Protect Human Health & Safety**
   - Ensuring that cleanup efforts minimize risks to visitors, staff, and wildlife.
   
2. **Enhance Access to Public Lands**
   - Improving accessibility and usability of park areas affected by contamination.

3. **Stimulate Economic Activity**
   - Promoting economic benefits through restoration and cleanup initiatives.

4. **Address Climate Change Impacts**
   - Mitigating the effects of environmental degradation exacerbated by climate change.

5. **Reduce Negative Impacts on Park Missions**
   - Aligning cleanup efforts with the core missions and values of national parks.

6. **Leverage Dollars Through Enhanced Collaboration**
   - Optimizing resources by partnering with external entities and leveraging available funds effectively.

7. **Shift Obligation to Another Public Entity**
   - Shifting responsibilities to other government bodies when feasible, thereby freeing up resources for other projects.

#### Subcriteria
Each of these main goals is broken down into more specific subcriteria that allow for detailed assessment and prioritization. For instance, under "Protect Human Health & Safety," subcriteria might include reducing exposure to hazardous substances and improving site safety measures. Similarly, under "Enhance Access to Public Lands," subcriteria could involve restoring trails, removing debris, and enhancing visitor amenities.

#### Source Policies
The criteria are supported by source policies, which provide guidelines and regulations that inform the decision-making process. These policies ensure that all projects adhere to established standards and comply with federal and state environmental laws.

#### Historical Context
The ECCD was established to address environmental issues that arose from past activities, such as mining operations and industrial practices, which have left behind contaminated sites within national parks. The ECCD aims to remediate these areas to restore ecological balance and protect public health.

#### Implementation and Challenges
Initially, the NPS faced challenges in allocating funds effectively due to a lack of prioritization and an overly dispersed approach. However, the introduction of the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) and Decision Lens software has enabled the NPS to establish a robust criteria-based system for prioritizing projects. This system allows for dynamic adjustments and the incorporation of multiple viewpoints, ensuring that resources are directed towards the most pressing and impactful cleanup efforts.

#### Example: Portfolio of Contaminated Sites
The ECCD manages a portfolio of over 3,000 contaminated sites across national parks. By employing the hierarchical model of criteria, the ECCD can systematically assess and prioritize these sites based on their potential to protect human health, enhance public access, and stimulate economic activity. This method ensures that limited resources are allocated efficiently, addressing the most critical issues first.

### Conclusion
The ECCD's hierarchical model of criteria serves as a comprehensive framework for prioritizing environmental cleanup projects within national parks. By breaking down broad goals into specific subcriteria and leveraging advanced decision-making tools, the NPS can effectively address historical contamination, protect public health, and enhance the recreational and economic value of these protected areas.
