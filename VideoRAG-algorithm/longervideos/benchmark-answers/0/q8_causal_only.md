### Caribou Migration: Navigating Vast Distances

Caribou undertake one of the most impressive migrations in the animal kingdom, traversing extensive landscapes over long periods. To successfully navigate these vast distances and maintain their course towards a specific destination, caribou rely on several navigational cues:

1. **Natural Landmarks:** Caribou use prominent features such as mountain ranges, rivers, and valleys to guide them along established migration paths. These landmarks serve as reference points that help the herd stay oriented throughout their journey.

2. **Seasonal Growth of Vegetation:** The video mentions that caribou chase the seasonal growth of grass. They are attuned to changes in vegetation, moving towards areas where plants are just beginning to grow and provide optimal nutrition. This natural cycle acts as a key cue for migration timing and direction.

3. **Solar Cues:** Like many migratory animals, caribou may use the position of the sun at different times of day or night to orient themselves. The angle and intensity of sunlight can help them determine their north-south orientation.

4. **Celestial Navigation:** In addition to solar cues, caribou might also utilize patterns in the stars for nighttime navigation. Observing celestial bodies like the North Star could assist in maintaining a consistent direction during nocturnal travel.

5. **Magnetic Fields:** Recent studies suggest that some migratory animals can detect Earth's magnetic field, using it as an internal compass to guide them accurately over long distances. Caribou might also possess this ability, helping them stay on course despite challenging terrains and environmental conditions.

6. **Genetic Memory and Cultural Transmission:** Young caribou inherit knowledge of migration routes through genetic memory from their parents and other herd members. Additionally, social learning plays a crucial role as older animals lead the way, teaching younger generations about safe pathways and seasonal changes.

7. **Environmental Cues:** Sensory perceptions such as temperature shifts, wind patterns, and even smells can indicate proximity to water sources or preferred grazing areas, aiding in maintaining their migratory path.

These cues collectively ensure that caribou can maintain their course through diverse terrains, ranging from snowy landscapes to rugged mountains, all aimed at reaching crucial habitats for survival.
