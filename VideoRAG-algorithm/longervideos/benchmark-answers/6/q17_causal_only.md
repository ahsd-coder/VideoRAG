### Time-Frequency Patterns Indicative of Singularities in Signals

Based on the retrieved information, Daubechies and other lecturers describe specific time-frequency patterns that are indicative of singularities in signals. These patterns are often observed in the context of signal processing and wavelet analysis, as detailed in various presentations and lectures:

1. **Localized Peaks in Time-Frequency Space**:
   - Daubechies mentions that localized peaks in the time-frequency plane can indicate singularities. These peaks are often associated with rapid changes in the signal, such as edges or abrupt discontinuities.

2. **Broadening in Frequency**:
   - During the analysis of signals, it is noted that singularities can cause broadening in the frequency domain. This means that the frequency components of the signal become spread out rather than being concentrated at specific frequencies.

3. **Interference Between Frequencies**:
   - Signals with singularities exhibit interference patterns in the time-frequency plane. These interferences occur when frequencies are close to each other, leading to overlapping or mixed frequency components that are hard to separate.

4. **Changes in Instantaneous Frequency**:
   - Singularities can cause changes in the instantaneous frequency of a signal. When analyzing signals, it is observed that singularities lead to non-constant instantaneous frequencies, meaning that the frequency content of the signal varies rapidly over time.

5. **Wavelet Transform Analysis**:
   - Through wavelet transform analysis, singularities appear as localized regions of high energy in the time-frequency plane. Wavelet coefficients corresponding to these regions tend to be significantly higher compared to the surrounding areas.

6. **Spectrogram Visualizations**:
   - Spectrograms, which are visual representations of the signal's time-frequency content, show distinct patterns for singularities. These patterns include localized bands or clusters of high-intensity regions that correspond to the singularities in the signal.

These patterns collectively provide a framework for identifying and analyzing singularities within signals using time-frequency analysis techniques. By examining these characteristics, researchers and practitioners can better understand the structure and behavior of complex signals in various applications, including medical diagnostics, audio processing, and telecommunications.
