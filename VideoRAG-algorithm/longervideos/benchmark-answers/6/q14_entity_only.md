### Specific Medical Application of Time-Frequency Analysis in Fetal Health

Ingrid Daubechies highlights a specific medical application of time-frequency analysis in the context of fetal health, which involves extracting fetal heartbeats from maternal electrocardiogram (ECG) data. This application aims to monitor fetal heart rates non-invasively, thereby improving diagnostic capabilities for detecting potential heart issues in fetuses.

#### Key Points:

1. **Non-Invasive Monitoring**:
   - The technique allows for the extraction of fetal heart signals from ECG data collected from the mother's abdomen. This non-invasive method avoids the risks associated with invasive procedures, such as placing probes directly on the fetus.

2. **Signal Extraction**:
   - The process involves sophisticated signal processing techniques to isolate the fetal heartbeat signal from the dominant maternal ECG signal. This is crucial because the fetal heartbeat is a very small signal with a very low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR).

3. **Diagnostic Importance**:
   - Currently, for fetuses suspected of having heart problems, the only way to measure heart activity during delivery is through invasive methods, which carry inherent risks. Non-invasive extraction of fetal heartbeats via signal analysis represents a safer alternative.

4. **Technical Details**:
   - The analysis relies on time-frequency localization techniques to distinguish between the high-frequency maternal heartbeat and the lower-frequency fetal heartbeat. Techniques such as the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) and de-shape STFT are employed to achieve this differentiation.

5. **Clinical Applications**:
   - The ability to accurately extract and analyze fetal heartbeats has significant clinical implications. It enables continuous monitoring of fetal heart rates, potentially allowing early detection of abnormalities and timely intervention.
