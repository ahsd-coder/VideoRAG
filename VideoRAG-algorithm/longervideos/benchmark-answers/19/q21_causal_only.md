### Bezos's Rationale for Not Negotiating the Purchase Price of The Washington Post

At The Economic Club of Washington, D.C., Jeff Bezos shared his experience of purchasing The Washington Post. When approached by Don Graham, the owner of The Washington Post, Bezos initially expressed hesitation due to his lack of experience in the newspaper industry. However, after multiple conversations, Don Graham persuaded Bezos of the potential benefits of acquiring the publication. 

Bezos’s decision to forego negotiations was rooted in his trust in Don Graham. According to the transcript:

> "I said, fine, I didn't negotiate with him, and I did no due diligence. And I wouldn't need to with Don."

This quote indicates that Bezos trusted Don Graham implicitly, believing that any deal proposed by him would be fair and aligned with the true value of the Washington Post. Bezos’s confidence stemmed from his longstanding relationship with Don Graham, whom he had known for 15 years and had developed a deep level of mutual respect and understanding. As Bezos put it:

> "And I wouldn't need to with Don."

Additionally, the asking price for the Washington Post was $250 million, and Bezos accepted this price without hesitation. When asked about the purchase price, Bezos stated:

> "Don, I asked him how much he wanted. He said $250."

In summary, Bezos chose not to negotiate the purchase price because of his strong trust in Don Graham and his belief that the asking price accurately reflected the value of the newspaper. This decision was emblematic of Bezos's approach to business transactions, where trust and relationships played a crucial role.
