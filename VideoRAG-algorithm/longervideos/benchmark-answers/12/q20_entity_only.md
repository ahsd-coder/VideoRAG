Question: Explain the process of making 'nang', the grilled bread frequently observed in Xinjiang, detailing ingredients, tools, and cooking techniques.

### Process of Making Nang (Grilled Bread) in Xinjiang

#### Ingredients
- **Dough**: Typically made from flour, water, yeast, and sometimes milk or eggs for added flavor and texture.
- **Seasonings**: Salt, sugar, and sometimes oil or butter to enhance taste.
- **Toppings**: Commonly include meat (such as lamb or beef) and green onions. These are often sprinkled on the dough before baking.

#### Tools and Equipment
- **Wooden Surface**: Used for kneading and shaping the dough.
- **Specialized Knife**: For slicing the freshly baked bread.
- **Tandoor Oven**: A traditional clay oven used for baking the nang. It is heated to high temperatures and retains heat efficiently.
- **Tongs**: To handle the bread safely during the cooking process.
- **Metal Tray or Skewers**: Sometimes used for holding the bread while it cooks in the oven.

#### Cooking Techniques
1. **Preparation**:
   - The dough is mixed and kneaded until smooth. It is then allowed to rise.
   - After rising, the dough is divided into smaller portions and shaped into flat rounds or ovals.

2. **Shaping and Seasoning**:
   - Each piece of dough is flattened and stretched to form the desired shape.
   - If using toppings, they are applied to the dough before baking.

3. **Baking**:
   - The shaped dough is placed into the preheated tandoor oven.
   - Using tongs, the bread is carefully lowered into the oven where it adheres to the hot walls.
   - The bread is left to cook, with the heat from the oven walls giving it a crispy outer layer and a soft interior.

4. **Cooking Time**:
   - The cooking process usually takes several minutes. The bread is monitored closely to ensure it does not burn.
   - Once done, the bread is removed using tongs and sliced immediately to serve.

5. **Serving**:
   - Nang is typically served hot, often accompanied by tea or used as a base for additional toppings like grilled meat or vegetables.

#### Cultural Context
- **Social Interaction**: The process of making and consuming nang is often a communal activity, reflecting the social fabric of Xinjiang's food culture.
- **Market Environment**: Nang is commonly sold at street food markets, where vendors interact with customers and showcase the bread's freshness and quality.

This detailed process highlights the craftsmanship and cultural significance of nang, a staple in Xinjiang's culinary landscape.
