### Addressing 'Cradle' Interference in X-ray Images of Panel Paintings

Daubechies's team tackled the challenge of 'cradle' interference in X-ray images of panel paintings through a combination of technical innovations and collaborative efforts. The cradle refers to a lattice of hardwood strips added to the back of a wooden panel to provide structural support and prevent warping. However, these cradles often appear prominently in X-ray images, obscuring the underlying artwork and complicating the analysis of cracks and other features.

#### Key Steps in Overcoming the Challenge

1. **Development of Image Processing Algorithms**: 
   - Daubechies's team developed sophisticated image processing algorithms to remove cradle artifacts from X-ray images. These algorithms were designed to distinguish between the cradle's wood grain and the original panel's wood grain.
   - The team leveraged machine learning techniques to automate the process of removing cradle artifacts, which previously required extensive manual effort using tools like Photoshop.

2. **Mathematical Analysis**:
   - Detailed mathematical analyses were conducted to understand the patterns and characteristics of cradle artifacts. This involved Bruno Cornelis's thesis work, which explored the intricacies of crack detection and analysis.
   - Mathematical models were created to identify and isolate cradle-related features from the rest of the image.

3. **Collaborative Efforts**:
   - The team collaborated with art conservators to refine and validate their methods. This collaboration ensured that the algorithms produced results that aligned with the expectations and requirements of the conservation community.
   - The team received grants to develop a user-friendly software package that could be made available in open-source form, facilitating broader adoption and collaboration among art conservators and signal processing groups.

#### Impact of the Work

1. **Enhanced Image Quality**:
   - The algorithms significantly improved the clarity of X-ray images, enabling clearer visualization of the underlying artwork and cracks.
   - This improvement facilitated more accurate and detailed analysis of panel paintings, aiding in the preservation and restoration efforts.

2. **Preservation and Restoration**:
   - By removing cradle artifacts, the team enabled better assessment of the structural integrity and condition of panel paintings.
   - This advancement helped conservators make informed decisions regarding the treatment and restoration of artworks, thereby contributing to their long-term preservation.

3. **Scientific Collaboration**:
   - The interdisciplinary approach fostered collaboration between mathematicians, art historians, and conservators, leading to innovative solutions and methodologies.
   - The open-source availability of the software encouraged further development and adaptation by the broader community, enhancing its utility and impact.

In summary, Daubechies's team addressed the challenge of cradle interference by developing advanced image processing techniques and collaborating closely with art conservators. Their work not only enhanced the quality of X-ray images but also contributed to the broader field of art conservation through improved preservation and restoration practices.
