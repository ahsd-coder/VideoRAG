### Collaboration Between Ingrid Daubechies and the North Carolina Museum of Art

#### Overview
Ingrid Daubechies, a renowned mathematician and Distinguished Professor at Duke University, has collaborated extensively with the North Carolina Museum of Art (NCMA) on various projects related to art conservation and digital reconstructions. Her work leverages mathematical principles to enhance the understanding and preservation of historical artworks.

#### Key Projects and Collaborations

1. **Virtual Aging/Rejuvenating of Paintings**
   - Daubechies worked on a project titled "Virtual aging and rejuvenating of paintings on panel," known as the Ghissi Project. This collaboration involved Duke University and the NCMA.
   - The project aimed to virtually age and rejuvenate paintings to better understand their deterioration processes and to develop methods for their preservation.

2. **Reunification of Artwork Panels**
   - NCMA has several panels from a Ghissi altarpiece. These panels were originally part of a larger altarpiece that was later dispersed. Through collaboration with NCMA, Daubechies and her team worked on efforts to reunite these panels digitally and physically.
   - The NCMA discovered that three panels were part of the same altarpiece and initiated efforts to bring them together for an exhibition.

3. **Digital Reconstructions**
   - Daubechies and her team, including artists and conservators like Charlotte Caspers, were involved in recreating missing panels. For instance, Charlotte Caspers was commissioned to recreate a missing panel for NCMA.
   - These reconstructions serve both educational and conservation purposes, allowing museums to showcase high-quality reproductions of masterpieces.

4. **Technical and Mathematical Contributions**
   - Daubechies utilized mathematical algorithms to analyze and restore historical artworks. This included techniques such as analyzing the spectra of paintings to infer missing colors, as demonstrated in her work with the Van Gogh Museum.
   - She also worked on projects that involved the analysis of fabric patterns and brush strokes, contributing to the attribution and authentication of artworks.

5. **Educational Outreach**
   - The collaboration has resulted in presentations and exhibitions that educate the public about the technical and scientific aspects of art conservation. These initiatives aim to bridge the gap between academic research and museum practices.

6. **Cross-Institutional Partnerships**
   - The collaboration extended beyond NCMA to involve other institutions such as Duke University, ETH Zurich, and the Weizmann Institute. This multidisciplinary approach facilitated innovative solutions in art conservation.

#### Conclusion
Ingrid Daubechies's collaboration with the North Carolina Museum of Art exemplifies the interdisciplinary approach to art conservation and restoration. By integrating mathematical techniques with traditional art historical methods, her work has significantly advanced the field, providing valuable insights and tools for the preservation of cultural heritage.
