### Concerns of the North Carolina Museum of Art (NCMA) Regarding the Reconstruction of the Gisi Altarpiece

The North Carolina Museum of Art (NCMA) had several concerns regarding the reconstruction of the Gisi Altarpiece, primarily centered around the authenticity and impact of the new panel:

1. **Authenticity Concerns**:
   - When considering the reconstruction of the missing panel, there was a fear that a new, shiny panel might overshadow the authenticity of the original 14th-century panels. This concern stemmed from the desire to preserve the historical integrity of the artwork.
   
2. **Visual Impact**:
   - The NCMA was wary that the newly reconstructed panel might visually dominate the overall composition, detracting from the aesthetic balance and historical context of the original pieces. There was a worry that viewers might focus predominantly on the new addition rather than appreciating the entire ensemble.

3. **Minimal Original Investment**:
   - Efforts were made to ensure that the new panel would not significantly differ from the existing ones. The goal was to integrate the new panel seamlessly while minimizing any noticeable differences that could compromise the authenticity of the reconstruction.

4. **Preservation and Display**:
   - There was a consideration about how to display the reconstructed panel alongside the original pieces without diminishing the historical value of the latter. The NCMA aimed to maintain the educational and aesthetic integrity of the exhibition.

These concerns reflect the NCMA's commitment to preserving the historical and artistic authenticity of the Gisi Altarpiece while attempting to reunite its fragmented panels. The museum sought to balance the desire for completeness with the need to respect the original artwork's historical significance.
