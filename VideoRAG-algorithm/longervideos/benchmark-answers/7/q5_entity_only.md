### Collaboration Between Ingrid Daubechies and the North Carolina Museum of Art

Ingrid Daubechies collaborated with the North Carolina Museum of Art (NCMA) on several projects that combined mathematical techniques with the study and preservation of art. The following summarizes the key aspects of their collaboration:

#### Projects and Techniques
- **Virtual Aging/Rejuvenating of Paintings**: Daubechies worked on a project known as the "Ghissi Project," which involved virtually aging and rejuvenating paintings on panel. This work allowed for the simulation of how paintings might have appeared at different points in time, providing valuable insights for art historians and conservators.
- **Analyzing Van Gogh Portraits**: One of the projects involved analyzing portraits painted by Vincent van Gogh during a specific period. The team focused on the texture and contrast of these paintings, discovering that removing certain elements (like contrast and luminance) revealed consistent spectral properties across high, medium, and low-cost paintings. This finding helped infer missing colors and contributed to a deeper understanding of Van Gogh’s techniques.
- **Recreating Missing Panels**: Collaborating with Charlotte Caspers, an artist and art conservator, Daubechies assisted in recreating a missing panel for a painting. This project aimed to complete artworks that were incomplete due to loss or damage, thereby enriching the museum's collections and providing educational resources.

#### Key Partners and Contributions
- **Charlotte Caspers**: An artist and art conservator who was commissioned to recreate the missing panel for a painting, enhancing the educational value of the museum’s exhibits.
- **Rachel Yin**: One of Daubechies’ students who contributed significantly to the project.
- **William Brown and Noel O’Connor**: Conservators at the NCMA who played a pivotal role in facilitating the collaboration and ensuring the practical application of the research.

#### Academic and Professional Impact
- **Serendipitous Encounters**: The collaboration began when Daubechies joined North Carolina and met William Brown and Noel O’Connor, who had previously heard about her work and were eager to collaborate.
- **Data Sharing and Openness**: The NCMA is noted for its willingness to share data and collaborate openly, which facilitated the success of the projects.
- **Lectures and Presentations**: Daubechies delivered talks and presentations at various venues, including the International Congress of Mathematicians (ICM 2018) and Duke University, discussing the outcomes and methodologies of these collaborations.

This collaboration exemplifies the interdisciplinary approach to art conservation and history, leveraging advanced mathematical techniques to enhance our understanding and appreciation of artworks.
