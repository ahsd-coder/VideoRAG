### Human Expertise in Mathematical Applications to Art Conservation

#### Introduction
Projects involving the application of mathematics to art conservation rely heavily on the integration of human expertise from various fields, including art history, conservation, and mathematics. This collaboration ensures that the mathematical models and computational techniques used are not only technically sound but also culturally and historically accurate. 

#### Examples of Collaboration

1. **Rick Johnson's Work on Automatic Thread Counting**
   - **Context**: Rick Johnson, an electrical engineer at Cornell University, developed methods for automatic thread counting in paintings using image processing techniques.
   - **Human Expertise**: Art historians and conservators provided critical insights into the historical context and materials used in the paintings, which guided the application of Johnson's mathematical techniques.
   - **Impact**: His work facilitated more precise analysis of the threads in paintings, aiding in the identification and documentation of artists' techniques and materials.

2. **Massimo Fornasier's Fresco Restoration Project**
   - **Context**: Massimo Fornasier worked on the reconstruction of frescoes at the Eremitani Church in Padua, Italy, which were destroyed during World War II.
   - **Human Expertise**: Art conservators contributed their knowledge of the original frescoes' colors and styles, which was essential for accurately reconstructing the lost artwork.
   - **Impact**: By combining mathematical models with historical data, the team was able to virtually restore the frescoes, providing a glimpse of the original artwork's appearance.

3. **Virtual Aging/Rejuvenating Paintings Project**
   - **Context**: Ingrid Daubechies collaborated with the North Carolina Museum of Art on a project to virtually age and rejuvenate paintings.
   - **Human Expertise**: Conservators and art historians played a crucial role in understanding the aging process of paintings and in validating the mathematical models used to simulate these processes.
   - **Impact**: This project allowed for the creation of virtual representations of how paintings might have appeared in their original state or after aging, offering valuable insights for both conservation and historical interpretation.

4. **Thread Counting for Attribution**
   - **Context**: Rick Johnson also worked on a project involving thread counting to differentiate original artworks from copies.
   - **Human Expertise**: Art historians and curators provided extensive knowledge about the stylistic and material characteristics of different artists and periods, which was used to develop and refine the mathematical algorithms.
   - **Impact**: The project enabled more accurate attributions and helped authenticate artworks, thereby enhancing our understanding of art history.

#### Conclusion
The success of these projects hinges on the seamless integration of human expertise with mathematical and computational techniques. Art conservators and historians bring invaluable insights into the historical and cultural context of artworks, ensuring that the mathematical models are applied appropriately and yield meaningful results. This collaboration underscores the importance of interdisciplinary approaches in advancing the field of art conservation.
