### Concerns of the North Carolina Museum of Art (NCMA) Regarding the Reconstruction of the Gisi Altarpiece

The North Carolina Museum of Art (NCMA) had several initial concerns regarding the reconstruction of the Gisi Altarpiece:

1. **Authenticity and Distinction**:
   - One major concern was the potential for the new panel to overshadow the authenticity of the original 14th-century panels when displayed together. There was a fear that the bright, beautiful appearance of the reconstructed panel might draw disproportionate attention, making it difficult for viewers to appreciate the genuine historical pieces.

2. **Historical Accuracy**:
   - Ensuring the accuracy and fidelity of the reconstruction was paramount. Given that the original panel was missing, there were uncertainties about how closely the new panel could replicate the original style and composition. The museum wanted to avoid any discrepancies that might detract from the historical integrity of the altarpiece.

3. **Educational Value**:
   - While the museum aimed to educate the public about the altarpiece, there was a need to balance this with the authenticity of the experience. The museum was cautious about how the new panel would impact the educational value of the exhibit, particularly in terms of providing accurate historical context.

4. **Technical Challenges**:
   - The technical process of reconstruction posed its own set of challenges. Using identical pigments and painting techniques to match the original work required meticulous attention to detail. The museum needed to ensure that the reconstructed panel would integrate seamlessly with the existing panels.

These concerns reflect the NCMA's commitment to preserving the historical and artistic integrity of the Gisi Altarpiece while also striving to present a cohesive and educational exhibit.
