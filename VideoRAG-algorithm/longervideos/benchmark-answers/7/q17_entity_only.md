Question: In addition to her work with the North Carolina Museum of Art, what other applications of mathematics to art conservation and scientific research did Daubechies discuss?

### Applications of Mathematics to Art Conservation and Scientific Research Discussed by Daubechies

#### Collaborative Work on Bones, Teeth, and Animation
- **Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** Ingrid Daubechies collaborated with individuals from diverse backgrounds, including mathematicians, biologists, and other professionals, to work on projects involving bones, teeth, and animation. This interdisciplinary approach highlights the broad applicability of mathematical techniques in various fields.
- **Affiliations:** The collaborators were affiliated with institutions such as ETH Zurich, Duke University, and Weizmann Institute. This collaborative effort underscores the importance of teamwork in advancing research.

#### Mathematical Tools for Image Processing and Analysis
- **Thread Counting for Paintings:** Daubechies discussed the use of mathematical tools for image processing and analysis, specifically mentioning Rick Johnson's work on "Automatic Thread Counting for Paintings on Canvas." This technique helps in attributing paintings based on the thread count, which can be a valuable tool for art historians and conservators.
- **Image Analysis Techniques:** The application of image analysis techniques, such as the use of high-resolution data, was emphasized. These techniques enable researchers to examine paintings in detail without causing damage, thereby preserving the artwork's integrity.

#### Virtual Aging/Rejuvenating of Paintings
- **Ghissi Project:** Daubechies mentioned the "Ghissi Project," a collaboration between Duke University and the North Carolina Museum of Art. This project involved the virtual aging and rejuvenation of paintings on panel, demonstrating how mathematics can be used to virtually restore and age artwork.
- **Restoration Techniques:** The project utilized mathematical algorithms to inpaint cracks and adjust colors, simulating the aging process or restoring the original appearance of paintings. This approach allows for non-invasive conservation techniques, ensuring that the original artwork remains unharmed.

#### Intersection of Mathematics and Art History
- **Frescoes Restoration:** Daubechies highlighted the work of Massimo Fornasier and colleagues in reconstructing destroyed frescoes at the Eremitani Church in Padua, Italy. This project exemplifies how mathematical methods can be employed to digitally reconstruct lost or damaged artworks.
- **Digital Restoration:** Through digital restoration, researchers can create virtual reconstructions of artworks that were destroyed, providing a means to preserve and study the original pieces.

#### Educational and Outreach Initiatives
- **Mathemalchemy Project:** Daubechies discussed the "Mathemalchemy" project, which aims to bring mathematical ideas to life through artistic installations. This initiative combines mathematical concepts with artistic expression to engage the public in mathematical thinking.
- **Collaborative Art Installations:** The project involves creating intricate paper sculptures and digital installations that illustrate mathematical concepts, showcasing the beauty and creativity inherent in mathematics.

These applications demonstrate the versatility and power of mathematics in art conservation and scientific research, enabling innovative approaches to studying, preserving, and restoring cultural heritage.
