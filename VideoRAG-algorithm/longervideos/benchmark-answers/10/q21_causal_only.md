Question: Beyond technical performance, what personal aspects should traders track in their journals for self-evaluation?

### Personal Aspects Traders Should Track in Their Journals

When evaluating their trading performance, traders often focus primarily on technical metrics such as win-loss percentages, profits, and losses. However, it is equally important to track personal aspects that can influence trading decisions and outcomes. Here are some key areas that traders should consider including in their journals:

1. **Emotional State**:
   - **Rating Feelings**: Traders can rate their emotional state on a scale of 1 to 10, where 1 represents feeling very uncertain or fearful about entering a trade, and 6 or higher indicates a more neutral or confident state.
   - **Mental Stability**: Tracking emotions can help traders identify patterns or triggers that affect their trading decisions and overall performance.

2. **Confidence Levels**:
   - **Desire to Enter Trades**: Assessing the level of desire to enter a trade can reveal whether a trader is driven by fear or confidence.
   - **Decision-Making Under Pressure**: Evaluating how confident a trader feels when placing a trade can help them understand their decision-making process and improve over time.

3. **Self-Review**:
   - **Mistakes and Corrections**: Documenting mistakes made during live trading and outlining actions taken to correct them is crucial for continuous improvement.
   - **Learning from Past Trades**: Reviewing past trades to identify errors and learning from them can significantly enhance future performance.

4. **Thought Processes**:
   - **Thoughts Before and After Trades**: Noting thoughts and feelings before and after a trade can provide insights into the trader's mindset and decision-making.
   - **Reflections on Trade Outcomes**: Reflecting on why a trade was executed or exited can help traders refine their strategies and improve their trading psychology.

5. **External Influences**:
   - **News Events**: Recording any significant news events or external factors that might have influenced a trade can help traders understand how such events impact their decision-making.
   - **Market Conditions**: Documenting market conditions at the time of trading can provide context for future analysis and strategy adjustments.

By incorporating these personal aspects into their journals, traders can gain a more comprehensive understanding of their performance and develop strategies to mitigate emotional and cognitive biases that can negatively impact their trading outcomes. This holistic approach to self-evaluation can lead to more disciplined and profitable trading practices.
