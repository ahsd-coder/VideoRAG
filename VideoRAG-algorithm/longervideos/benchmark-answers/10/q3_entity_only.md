Question: How does the instructor differentiate between a 'buy limit' order and a 'buy stop' order?

### Differentiating Between 'Buy Limit' and 'Buy Stop' Orders

Instructors often explain the distinction between a 'buy limit' order and a 'buy stop' order by focusing on the specific price levels at which these orders are executed:

- **Buy Limit Order:** 
  - A 'buy limit' order instructs the broker to buy a security at a specified price that is lower than the current market price. For instance, if the current market price is $100, a 'buy limit' order would be placed at a price lower than $100, such as $95.
  - This type of order ensures that the trader buys at a lower price, which is beneficial when the trader believes the price will rise but wants to avoid paying the current higher price.
  - Example from the video transcripts: "A buy limit order is telling your broker to get in at a price that's lower than we're currently trading."

- **Buy Stop Order:**
  - A 'buy stop' order, conversely, is placed at a price that is higher than the current market price. When the market price reaches this higher price, the order is executed.
  - This type of order is typically used as a protective measure or to capitalize on a potential upward trend. If the trader believes the price will continue to rise after reaching a certain level, they might place a 'buy stop' order above the current price.
  - Example from the video transcripts: "A buy stop order is saying to your broker, if we hit this level above what we're trading, I want to buy."

These definitions are consistently reinforced through various video segments, emphasizing the importance of understanding these order types for effective trading strategies.
