Question: How does Saint-Gobain's organizational structure aid its sustainability goals?

### Saint-Gobain's Organizational Structure and Sustainability Goals

#### Reorganization for National Disciplines
Saint-Gobain underwent a significant reorganization approximately 20 years ago, led by Paul Johnston, the former CEO. This restructuring shifted the company from a geographical-based model to one organized around national disciplines. This change aimed to foster multidisciplinary collaboration and growth, allowing the company to better address sustainability goals by integrating various departments and expertise.

#### Centralized Research and Development (R&D)
The company allocates substantial resources to R&D, investing nearly $600 million annually globally. Saint-Gobain operates through six corporate R&D centers strategically located in major developed markets such as the United States, France, and Germany. This centralized approach enables the company to leverage collective knowledge and innovations, accelerating the development of sustainable products and technologies.

#### Focus on Country-Based Leadership
Saint-Gobain places a strong emphasis on country-based leadership, appointing native CEOs in each country. This localization ensures that the company can tailor its sustainability initiatives to meet specific regional needs and challenges. For instance, a CEO in Brazil can focus on local environmental concerns and sustainable practices relevant to the region.

#### Strategic Initiatives and Environmental Responsibility
The company's strategic initiatives are closely aligned with sustainability goals. Key areas of focus include:

- **Energy Efficiency and Retrofitting:** Addressing the need to improve energy efficiency in existing buildings and retrofitting them to reduce CO2 emissions.
- **Natural Resource Preservation:** Implementing measures to conserve natural resources and ensure sustainable use.
- **Light Construction:** Developing lightweight materials that reduce the carbon footprint of construction projects.

#### Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) Committee
Saint-Gobain has established a CSR committee chaired by the CEO, which oversees the company's commitment to sustainability. This committee ensures that sustainability is integrated into all aspects of the company's operations and decision-making processes.

#### Internal Carbon Pricing
Since 2016, Saint-Gobain has implemented an internal carbon pricing mechanism to incentivize sustainable practices across its operations. This tool helps the company quantify and manage its environmental impact, driving further reductions in CO2 emissions.

#### Community Engagement and Collaboration
The company actively engages with communities and stakeholders to drive sustainability initiatives. For example, it collaborates with universities like Columbia to share knowledge and best practices, fostering a culture of innovation and environmental stewardship.

#### Example: Hydrogen Use in Glass Furnaces
Saint-Gobain has demonstrated its commitment to sustainability by pioneering the use of hydrogen in glass furnaces, a challenging but promising approach to reduce environmental impact. This initiative underscores the company's willingness to explore innovative solutions despite technical difficulties.

In summary, Saint-Gobain's organizational structure supports its sustainability goals through a combination of localized leadership, centralized R&D, strategic initiatives, and a strong commitment to environmental responsibility. These efforts collectively contribute to the company's mission of making the world a better home.
