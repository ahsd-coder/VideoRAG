Question: Describe the two phases involved in implementing absolute measurement in SuperDecisions software.

### Implementing Absolute Measurement in SuperDecisions Software

Absolute measurement in the SuperDecisions software is implemented in two distinct phases:

1. **Creating Rating Scales:**
   - **Step 1:** Begin by defining rating scales for each criterion. This involves specifying a range of values that can be assigned to different aspects of the alternatives being evaluated. For example, criteria such as "Prestige," "Price," "Miles Per Gallon (MPG)," and "Comfort" can have their own unique rating scales.
   - **Step 2:** Users can either create new scales or load existing ones. For instance, a user might create a custom scale for MPG based on known car profile data, assigning precise numerical ranges such as 29 to 32 and 22 to 30.
   - **Step 3:** After defining the scales, users can compare the scale items pairwise to assign numerical values that reflect their relative importance or performance. This process ensures that each criterion is appropriately quantified and ready for evaluation.

2. **Entering Judgments and Evaluating Alternatives:**
   - **Step 1:** Once the scales are defined, users enter judgments for each alternative under each criterion. This involves rating each alternative based on the criteria established in the first phase.
   - **Step 2:** Users can choose from various methods to enter these judgments, including graphical, verbal, or matrix modes. For example, in the graphical mode, users can visually select components like nodes and adjust values without needing to save every time.
   - **Step 3:** The software synthesizes the entered judgments to generate comprehensive rankings based on the criteria. Users can view the synthesized results in different formats, such as tables or graphical representations, to understand the comparative strengths and weaknesses of each alternative.

By following these two phases, the SuperDecisions software enables users to handle large numbers of alternatives efficiently, avoiding the need for extensive pairwise comparisons that would otherwise be time-consuming and cumbersome.
