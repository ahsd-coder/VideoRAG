Question: How does the process of synthesizing a model with sub-criteria differ from synthesizing a model without sub-criteria in SuperDecisions?

### Process of Synthesizing Models with Sub-Criteria vs Without Sub-Criteria in SuperDecisions

#### Synthesizing a Model with Sub-Criteria

1. **Hierarchical Structure**: 
   - In models with sub-criteria, the hierarchical structure is more complex, often including multiple levels of criteria and sub-criteria.
   - The goal node is connected to first-level criteria, which are further connected to second-level sub-criteria, and so forth.

2. **Creating and Organizing Clusters**:
   - The process starts with creating clusters for the goal, criteria, and sub-criteria.
   - For instance, in the fishery resource allocation model, clusters are created for goals such as "Rebuild and Maintain," "Forecast," "Protect Species," and "Seafood Safety."
   
3. **Entering Judgments**:
   - Pairwise comparisons are made between nodes at each level, ensuring consistency in judgments.
   - For example, in the car selection model, criteria like "Price," "Quality," and "Comfort" are compared, and sub-criteria like "Driving Performance" and "Ride" are compared under the "Comfort" criterion.
   
4. **Defining Rating Scales**:
   - Rating scales are defined for each criterion and sub-criterion, providing a basis for evaluating alternatives.
   - In the nuclear waste disposal model, criteria such as "State of technology" and "Health, safety, and environmental impacts" have sub-criteria like "Short-term radiological safety" and "Long-term radiological safety."

5. **Adding Alternatives**:
   - Alternatives are added under the criteria or sub-criteria clusters, depending on the model’s design.
   - For instance, in the car selection model, alternatives like "Toyota Camry" and "Honda Civic" are added under the criteria clusters.

6. **Synthesis Process**:
   - The synthesis process involves computing priorities for each level of criteria and sub-criteria.
   - This often requires more computational steps to ensure that all levels are accurately reflected in the final priorities.
   - In the fishery management model, the synthesis process considers all sub-criteria to determine the overall priorities for fishery projects.

7. **Reviewing Results**:
   - Once synthesized, the results are reviewed to ensure accuracy and consistency.
   - This may involve checking for any inconsistencies or errors in the judgments and ratings.

#### Synthesizing a Model Without Sub-Criteria

1. **Simplified Hierarchical Structure**:
   - Models without sub-criteria have a simpler hierarchical structure, with only the goal node connected directly to criteria.
   - For example, in a basic car selection model, criteria like "Price," "Quality," and "Comfort" are directly connected to the goal node without further breakdown into sub-criteria.

2. **Creating Clusters**:
   - Similar to models with sub-criteria, clusters are created for the goal and criteria.
   - However, there is no need to create clusters for sub-criteria.

3. **Pairwise Comparisons**:
   - Pairwise comparisons are made between criteria nodes, and judgments are entered directly.
   - In the car selection model, pairwise comparisons are made between criteria like "Price" and "Quality."

4. **Rating Scales**:
   - Rating scales are defined for each criterion, similar to models with sub-criteria, but without the additional layers of sub-criteria.
   - For example, a rating scale is defined for "Price" to evaluate different car models.

5. **Adding Alternatives**:
   - Alternatives are added under the criteria clusters.
   - In the car selection model, car models like "Toyota Camry" and "Honda Civic" are added under the criteria clusters.

6. **Synthesis Process**:
   - The synthesis process is simpler as it involves computing priorities for the criteria directly connected to the goal.
   - This process requires fewer computational steps compared to models with sub-criteria.

7. **Reviewing Results**:
   - Reviewing the results focuses on ensuring the accuracy of the criteria priorities and their impact on the overall goal.
   - This review process is generally quicker and simpler compared to models with sub-criteria.

### Summary

- **Complexity**: Models with sub-criteria are more complex and require more detailed hierarchical structures and computational steps.
- **Simplicity**: Models without sub-criteria are simpler and involve fewer steps in the synthesis process.
- **Result Accuracy**: Both types of models aim for accurate and consistent results, but models with sub-criteria may offer more nuanced and detailed evaluations.

By understanding these differences, users can better tailor their models in SuperDecisions to suit the complexity and detail required for their decision-making processes.
