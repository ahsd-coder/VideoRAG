### Converting an AHP Model to an ANP Model: Adding New Connections and Interdependence

#### Overview
Converting an AHP (Analytic Hierarchy Process) model to an ANP (Analytic Network Process) model involves several steps, primarily focusing on the addition of new connections that reflect the interdependence between criteria and alternatives. In an AHP model, criteria are hierarchically structured and compared independently, whereas in an ANP model, criteria and alternatives are interconnected, forming a network that captures the interdependencies among them.

#### Steps to Convert AHP to ANP

1. **Remove the Goal Node**:
   - Start by removing the goal node from the AHP model. ANP models do not have a goal node since they are based on a network structure rather than a hierarchical one.
   
2. **Add Connections from Alternatives to Criteria**:
   - One of the critical steps in the conversion is adding connections from each alternative to every criterion. This step is crucial as it establishes a two-way interaction between criteria and alternatives, which is a core principle of the ANP model.
   - **Example**: In a car selection model, you would connect each car (alternative) to criteria such as "price," "fuel efficiency," and "comfort."

3. **Establish Interconnections Between Criteria**:
   - Unlike AHP, where criteria are independent, ANP requires establishing connections between criteria to capture their mutual influences. For instance, "price" might influence "fuel efficiency," and vice versa.
   - **Example**: In a fishery resource allocation model, "good fish quality" might influence "habitat conservation," and "seafood safety" could depend on "management."

4. **Maintain Consistency**:
   - Ensure that all connections are consistent and logical. This involves checking for any loops or redundant connections that could distort the model’s integrity.
   - **Example**: Avoid creating a loop where a criterion influences itself directly or indirectly.

#### Reflecting Interdependence

- **Two-Way Relationships**:
  - In ANP, criteria and alternatives are not just compared hierarchically but also interact with each other in a network structure. This means that each criterion can influence and be influenced by other criteria and alternatives.
  
- **Dynamic Sensitivity Analysis**:
  - ANP supports dynamic sensitivity analysis, which allows for the examination of how changes in one criterion affect the priorities of other criteria and alternatives. This is particularly useful in understanding the robustness of the model and the impact of varying priorities.

- **Visual Representation**:
  - The network structure of ANP can be visually represented using nodes and connections, making it easier to identify and manage interdependencies. Software tools like SuperDecisions facilitate the creation and management of these networks.
