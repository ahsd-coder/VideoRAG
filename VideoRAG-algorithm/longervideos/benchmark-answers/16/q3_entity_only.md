Question: How is the unweighted supermatrix used in AHP, and how is it generated in Super Decisions?

### Unweighted Supermatrix in AHP and Its Generation in Super Decisions

#### Overview of Unweighted Supermatrix in AHP

In the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), the unweighted supermatrix plays a critical role in consolidating and synthesizing the judgments made at various levels of the hierarchy. This supermatrix captures the interdependencies and priorities of criteria and alternatives in a structured format, allowing for the computation of final priorities for decision-making.

#### Steps to Generate the Unweighted Supermatrix in Super Decisions

1. **Creating the Model:**
   - Start by creating a hierarchical structure within Super Decisions, which includes a goal node, criteria nodes, and alternative nodes. This structure is typically organized in three levels: the goal, criteria, and alternatives.
   - Use the software to input the necessary data, such as pairwise comparisons and ratings, for each level of the hierarchy.

2. **Inputting Pairwise Comparisons and Ratings:**
   - Enter judgments for pairwise comparisons between criteria and alternatives. These judgments reflect the relative importance or preference of one element over another.
   - Utilize the graphical, verbal, or matrix modes within Super Decisions to input these judgments accurately.

3. **Generating Local Priorities:**
   - Once the judgments are entered, the software computes local priorities for each criterion and alternative based on the pairwise comparisons. These local priorities are essential for forming the unweighted supermatrix.

4. **Constructing the Unweighted Supermatrix:**
   - After the local priorities are calculated, Super Decisions constructs the unweighted supermatrix. This matrix consists of blocks corresponding to each criterion and alternative.
   - Each block contains the local priorities derived from the pairwise comparisons, arranged in a way that reflects the hierarchical structure.

5. **Synthesizing the Model:**
   - Click on the "Synthesize" option within the software to process the unweighted supermatrix and derive the final priorities for the alternatives.
   - This synthesis step combines the local priorities across all levels of the hierarchy to produce a comprehensive ranking of alternatives.

#### Visual Interface and Navigation

- **Navigation Tabs:** The interface includes tabs such as "Cluster Node," "Make Subnetwork," and "Remove Subnetwork," which help in organizing and managing the hierarchical structure.
- **Node Connections:** Use the "Node connections from 'Goal'" dialog box to ensure that all criteria and alternatives are correctly connected to the goal node.
- **Judgment Sections:** Navigate to the "Judgments" tab to input judgments for pairwise comparisons and ratings.

#### Example Application

Consider a scenario where the goal is to select the best car from options like Acura TL, Toyota Camry, and Honda Civic. Criteria such as prestige, price, miles per gallon (MPG), and comfort are used to evaluate these alternatives. The process involves:
- Inputting judgments for pairwise comparisons between criteria and alternatives.
- Generating local priorities for each criterion and alternative.
- Constructing the unweighted supermatrix to consolidate these priorities.
- Synthesizing the model to obtain the final priorities and ranking of alternatives.

By following these steps, you can effectively generate and utilize the unweighted supermatrix in Super Decisions for informed decision-making based on AHP principles.
