Question: How can the results of AHP analysis be visualized in Super Decisions, including the priorities of alternatives and criteria?

### Visualization of AHP Analysis Results in Super Decisions

The Super Decisions software provides a range of visualization tools to help interpret and present the results of Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) analysis. Here's how you can visualize the priorities of alternatives and criteria:

#### 1. **Graphical Representation**
   - **Bar Charts**: One common method is using bar charts to display the priorities of alternatives and criteria. These charts provide a straightforward visual comparison of the relative importance of each alternative or criterion.
     - Example: When comparing car models like Acura TL, Toyota Camry, and Honda Civic, the bar chart will show the normalized priorities for each car, allowing you to easily identify the best choice based on your criteria.

#### 2. **Network Diagrams**
   - **Hierarchical Structure**: Super Decisions allows you to visualize the hierarchical structure of your decision-making model. This includes the goal node, criteria nodes, and alternative nodes, connected in a network diagram.
     - Example: A three-level hierarchy where the goal is to "buy the best car," criteria such as "prestige," "price," "miles per gallon," and "comfort" are connected to the goal, and specific car models like Acura TL, Toyota Camry, and Honda Civic are listed as alternatives.

#### 3. **Tabular Data**
   - **Priority Tables**: You can view the priorities of alternatives and criteria in tabular form. This includes columns for "Graphic," "Ideals," and "Normals," providing numerical values that represent the priorities.
     - Example: The software generates a table with alternatives listed in one column and their corresponding normalized scores in other columns, enabling you to see the precise priority values for each alternative.

#### 4. **Sensitivity Analysis**
   - **Dynamic Sensitivity**: This feature helps understand how changes in criteria weights affect the priorities of alternatives. Visual representations like 3D bar charts can illustrate the impact of varying criterion weights on alternative priorities.
     - Example: Using a 3D bar chart, you can see how the priorities of Acura TL, Toyota Camry, and Honda Civic shift as you modify the weight of criteria like "cost" or "fuel efficiency."

#### 5. **Synthesized Results**
   - **Final Priority Scores**: After synthesizing the model, you can view the final priority scores for each alternative. This consolidates the individual priorities from all criteria into a single, comprehensive score.
     - Example: Once you synthesize the whole model, the software will display the overall priority scores for each car model, indicating the best choice based on the combined criteria.

#### 6. **Export Options**
   - **Clipboard and Excel**: You can export the priority tables and other data to the clipboard or an Excel spreadsheet for further analysis or presentation.
     - Example: After synthesizing the model, you can copy the table containing the priorities of alternatives and criteria to an Excel sheet, facilitating easier sharing and reporting.

### Steps to Visualize Results in Super Decisions

1. **Open the Software Interface**:
   - Navigate to the "Super Decisions Main Window" and ensure your model is loaded.

2. **Access Computation Tools**:
   - Go to the "Computations" section to synthesize the model and generate the priorities.

3. **View Graphical Representations**:
   - Use the "Graphical View" option to see bar charts and network diagrams representing the priorities.

4. **Perform Sensitivity Analysis**:
   - Utilize the "Sensitivity Analysis" feature to explore how changes in criteria weights affect the priorities.

5. **Export Data**:
   - Copy the priority tables to the clipboard or export them to an Excel spreadsheet for further use.

By leveraging these visualization tools and steps, you can effectively communicate the results of your AHP analysis, making it easier to understand and share the decision-making process and outcomes.
