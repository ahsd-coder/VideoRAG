Question: Why is absolute measurement preferred over relative measurement when dealing with a large number of alternatives in AHP?

### Absolute Measurement vs. Relative Measurement in AHP

Absolute measurement is often preferred over relative measurement when dealing with a large number of alternatives in the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) for several reasons:

1. **Efficiency and Practicality**:
   - When there are numerous alternatives, the process of pairwise comparisons required for relative measurement becomes cumbersome and time-consuming. Absolute measurement allows for more straightforward and efficient evaluation of alternatives by assigning absolute ratings to each alternative based on predefined criteria.

2. **Accuracy and Objectivity**:
   - With absolute measurement, each alternative is evaluated independently against established criteria, reducing the risk of subjective biases that can arise from comparing alternatives directly against each other. This method ensures that each alternative is assessed based on its own merits rather than its relative position.

3. **Handling Large Data Sets**:
   - As mentioned in the tutorial videos, absolute measurement processes are more suitable when dealing with a large number of alternatives. For instance, the video tutorial discusses how absolute measurement is used in the context of MBA admissions, where a large pool of candidates is evaluated based on criteria such as ATR (Admission Test Results), CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average), WE (Work Experience), LOR (Letter of Recommendation), and EA (Extra Activities).

4. **Software Support**:
   - Software tools like SuperDecisions support the implementation of absolute measurement processes, making it feasible to manage and analyze a large number of alternatives effectively. The tutorials demonstrate how to use SuperDecisions to define rating scales, enter judgments, and synthesize results for large sets of alternatives.

5. **Avoiding Comparative Biases**:
   - Direct pairwise comparisons can introduce biases, especially when the number of alternatives is large. Absolute measurement helps mitigate these biases by focusing on the intrinsic qualities of each alternative rather than their relative positions.

In summary, absolute measurement in AHP is preferred for large datasets due to its efficiency, objectivity, and ability to handle extensive data sets effectively. This method leverages software tools like SuperDecisions to streamline the evaluation process, ensuring that each alternative is assessed accurately and comprehensively.
