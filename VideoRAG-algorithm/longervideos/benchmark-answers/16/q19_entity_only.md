Question: What are the different visualization options available in SuperDecisions for displaying sensitivity analysis results?

### Visualization Options for Sensitivity Analysis in SuperDecisions

When conducting sensitivity analysis in SuperDecisions, there are several visualization options available to help users understand the results:

1. **Plots**:
   - Plots provide a straightforward way to visualize how the priorities of alternatives change as the weight of a criterion varies. These plots typically display data points and lines that indicate trends and reversals in ranking based on changes in criterion weight.

2. **Bar Charts**:
   - Bar charts are used to compare the priorities of different alternatives visually. Users can change parameters to see how the bars representing each alternative shift, offering a clear picture of how sensitive the priorities are to changes in criterion weight.

3. **Pie Charts**:
   - Pie charts break down the priorities into segments, making it easier to see the relative proportions of each alternative. Changing parameters can alter the size of each segment, illustrating how the distribution of priorities changes.

4. **Horizontal Bar Charts**:
   - Similar to regular bar charts, horizontal bar charts orient the bars horizontally, which can sometimes make comparisons clearer depending on the specific data and the user's preference.

5. **3D Bar Charts**:
   - 3D bar charts provide a more immersive and detailed view of the priorities. They can be particularly useful for showcasing complex datasets with multiple criteria and alternatives, allowing users to rotate and examine the data from different angles.

6. **Line Graphs**:
   - Line graphs are useful for tracking trends over continuous variables, such as the weight of a criterion. They can show how the priorities evolve as the weight changes, highlighting any critical points where priorities reverse.

These visualization options allow users to explore the sensitivity of their decision-making models comprehensively, helping them identify which criteria are most influential and how changes in those criteria affect the overall priorities of alternatives.
