Question: How does the symbiotic relationship between saddleback clownfish and carpet anemones benefit both species?

### Symbiotic Relationship Between Saddleback Clownfish and Carpet Anemones

The interaction between saddleback clownfish (Amphiprion polymnus) and carpet anemones is a classic example of mutualism, where both the fish and the anemone benefit from their relationship.

#### Benefits for the Clownfish
1. **Protection**: The tentacles of carpet anemones are toxic to most marine animals but harmless to clownfish due to their immunity to the anemone's poison. This allows clownfish to use the anemone as a refuge, protecting them from predators.
2. **Shelter**: Within the anemone’s tentacles, the fish can find shelter and hide from threats such as larger fish and invertebrates.
3. **Feeding**: The fish often feed on leftover bits of food or parasites that may be present around the anemone.

#### Benefits for the Anemone
1. **Cleaning Service**: Clownfish help clean the anemone by removing debris, dead tentacles, and parasites from its surface.
2. **Attracting Prey**: The presence of clownfish can attract small fish to the area, potentially serving as a lure for prey that the anemone can then capture.

This relationship showcases how both species benefit in significant ways:

- **Anemones**: Improved health through cleaning activities by clownfish.

This mutualistic interaction underscores the delicate balance and interdependence of marine life, contributing to the overall ecological stability of coral reef ecosystems.
