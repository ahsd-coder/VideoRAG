Question: What challenges do puffin parents face when raising their chicks, and how have declining fish numbers impacted their ability to provide for their offspring?

### Challenges Faced by Puffin Parents

Puffin parents encounter several significant challenges while raising their chicks. One major challenge is the necessity of frequent trips to gather food. These journeys often involve long distances as they must fly up to 50 kilometers out to sea to find sufficient fish, especially when local stocks are low.

#### Declining Fish Populations
The scarcity of fish populations has exacerbated these difficulties for puffin parents:
- **Increased Distance and Effort**: With declining fish numbers in their immediate vicinity, puffins frequently have to travel longer distances to locate adequate food sources. This means that parents spend more time away from their nests, sometimes up to three hours per round trip.
  
#### Food Competition
Another challenge is the competition for available resources:
- **Intense Competition**: As shown in video segments featuring puffin flocks flying over the ocean or diving into the sea, the struggle for food becomes highly competitive. When fish are scarce, finding enough sustenance can be a matter of survival.

#### Environmental Changes
Environmental changes also pose significant threats to puffins' ability to secure food:
- **Impact on Fishing Grounds**: Reduced fish stocks in traditional fishing grounds force puffin parents to venture further into the open sea where food is more plentiful but less reliable.
  
### Coexistence with Other Marine Species

Puffins share their marine environment with other species, often competing for limited resources. This competition intensifies when food availability decreases:
- **Cooperation and Conflict**: While puffins may engage in cooperative feeding behaviors as seen in aerial scenes of large flocks soaring over the ocean, they also face conflicts with other seabirds hunting in similar areas.

### Conclusion
The primary challenges faced by puffin parents include finding sufficient food amidst declining fish populations and competing for limited resources. These difficulties underscore the vulnerability of puffins to environmental changes, making it harder for them to ensure their chicks' survival and growth.
