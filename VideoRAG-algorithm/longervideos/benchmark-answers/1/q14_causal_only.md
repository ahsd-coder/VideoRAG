Question: How does the Portuguese man-of-war capture and consume its prey?

### Portuguese Man-of-War Predation Mechanism

The **Portuguese man-of-war** is a fascinating marine predator known for its complex structure and lethal tentacles. Here’s how it captures and consumes its prey:

1. **Lethal Tentacles**: The Portuguese man-of-war possesses long, slender tentacles that can extend up to 30 meters in length. Each of these tentacles is armed with thousands of tiny stinging cells called nematocysts. When prey comes into contact with the tentacles, it triggers a rapid release of venomous harpoons from the nematocysts.

2. **Capture and Immobilization**:
   - As prey such as fish or small crustaceans are paralyzed by the sting, the man-of-war begins to reel in the struggling victim towards its body using specialized muscular tentacles.
   - These muscular structures are capable of grabbing and holding onto prey tightly once it is captured.

3. **Transfer to Digestive Structures**:
   - Once a fish or another small marine creature is immobilized, the man-of-war uses additional tentacles with specialized muscles to transfer the victim from the capture tentacles to areas dedicated for digestion.
   - This process ensures that the prey can be efficiently moved and prepared for consumption.

4. **Chemical Digestion**:
   - The digestive system of the Portuguese man-of-war includes powerful enzymes capable of liquefying the captured prey. These chemicals break down the victim's tissues into a semi-liquid form, making it easier to absorb.
   
5. **Consumption and Remnants**:
   - After the prey is fully digested by these chemical processes, only remnants are left behind. This method ensures that no part of the prey goes to waste.
