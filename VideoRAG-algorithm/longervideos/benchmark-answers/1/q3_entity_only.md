Question: How do poison dart frogs care for their young?

### Care of Poison Dart Frog Young

Poison dart frogs exhibit intricate behaviors when caring for their offspring, ensuring their survival through various stages from eggs to tadpoles.

#### Egg-Laying and Early Stages:
- **Egg Placement:** A female lays a single unfertilized egg on a leaf or other suitable surface. 
- **Fertilization:** The male then fertilizes the egg.
- **Nutrition:** The mother provides nutrition for her tadpole by depositing unfertilized eggs on leaves near the tadpole, serving as sustenance.

#### Parental Roles:
- **Male Responsibility:** Males typically play a crucial role in raising their young. They are responsible for transporting and placing the developing tadpoles into small water pools or microhabitats.
- **Tadpole Placement:** The father will place each of his tadpoles in its own tiny pool of water, ensuring that they have safe locations to develop.

#### Tadpole Development:
- **Safety First:** Each tadpole is placed in a different puddle to minimize competition and increase survival rates. This process involves the male remembering where he put each tadpole.
- **Water Management:** Parents continue their rounds for six weeks, visiting these pools regularly to lay additional unfertilized eggs as food for the growing tadpoles.

#### Challenges:
- **Environmental Adaptation:** Some tadpoles might need relocation due to environmental changes such as drying conditions. The male must find a better place for them to ensure survival.
- **Parental Care Over Time:** Throughout this period, both parents work together diligently, demonstrating teamwork and memory to care for their offspring until they are ready to undergo metamorphosis.

#### Conclusion:
Poison dart frogs exhibit complex parenting behaviors that involve meticulous attention to detail and dedication over an extended period. These efforts ensure the survival of their young through critical stages from egg-laying to tadpole development into adult frogs.
