### Key Differences Between Consensus GPT and Scholar GPT

Based on the available information, here are the key differences between Consensus GPT and Scholar GPT:

#### **Consensus GPT**
- **Purpose**: Consensus GPT is an AI Research Assistant designed to search through a vast database of academic papers, typically around 200 million papers. 
- **Functionality**: It provides science-based answers and drafts content with accurate citations, ensuring that the information is grounded in reliable academic sources.
- **Performance**: The model is noted for its ability to accurately summarize findings and provide detailed explanations based on human Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs) from top journals, as demonstrated in discussions about fish oil's impact on mood.
- **Limitations**: It may face issues related to "hallucination," where it can sometimes generate incorrect or invented information without proper basis.

#### **Scholar GPT**
- **Purpose**: Scholar GPT is another AI Research Assistant with access to a larger database compared to Consensus GPT. 
- **Database Size**: It has access to a much larger database, which includes sources like Google Scholar and PubMed, suggesting a broader scope of information.
- **Functionality**: Similar to Consensus GPT, it assists in literature review, citation management, and writing support. However, its larger database allows it to provide more comprehensive and diverse information.
- **Customization**: Scholar GPT is highlighted for its potential to be customized with proprietary data, which could enhance its effectiveness and personalization in academic and professional tasks.

#### **Comparisons**
- **Database Access**: While Consensus GPT accesses 200 million academic papers, Scholar GPT has access to a much larger database, including Google Scholar and PubMed, providing a wider range of information.
- **Proprietary Data**: Scholar GPT is noted for its ability to leverage proprietary data, which can make it more effective and personalized compared to Consensus GPT.
- **Usage Examples**: Both models are used for tasks such as literature review, citation management, and writing support, but Scholar GPT's larger database and potential for proprietary data integration make it stand out in terms of breadth and depth of information.

### Conclusion
Both Consensus GPT and Scholar GPT serve similar purposes as AI Research Assistants but differ in their database sizes and potential for customization. Scholar GPT, with its access to a larger database and capability to incorporate proprietary data, offers a more comprehensive and personalized research assistance experience.
