Question: What industry does Elon Musk compare the production of humanoid robots to in terms of global capacity?

### Comparison of Production Capacities

Elon Musk compares the production of humanoid robots to the automobile industry in terms of global capacity. Specifically, he mentions that the global capacity for producing vehicles is around 100 million units per year, which he suggests is a comparable metric to consider for the mass production of humanoid robots.

In the retrieved video content, Musk discusses the analogy between vehicle production and humanoid robots:

> **Video Clip 1:**  
> *Speaker 1 (in black t-shirt):* "It's the same as cars, really. I mean, global capacity for vehicles is about 100 million a year. And it could be higher. It's just that the demand is on the order of 100 million a year and then there's roughly 2 billion vehicles that are in use in some way."

This comparison underscores the potential scale of production for humanoid robots, suggesting that existing industrial capabilities and demands within the automotive sector can serve as a reference point for understanding the logistics and feasibility of mass-producing humanoid robots. 

### Context and Implications

Musk's comments highlight the notion that the infrastructure, supply chains, and manufacturing processes established for vehicle production can be adapted for humanoid robots. This implies that the challenges and solutions for scaling up production in the automotive industry could offer valuable insights for the emerging field of humanoid robotics. 

Additionally, the discussion touches upon the demand for such robots, indicating that the current market for vehicles provides a benchmark for estimating the potential demand for humanoid robots once they become widely available and useful.

By drawing parallels between these industries, Musk aims to contextualize the potential scale and impact of humanoid robots in the broader landscape of manufacturing and technological advancement.
